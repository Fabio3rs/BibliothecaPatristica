SUBJECT DISCOVERY AND ARGUMENT CHECK — concepts-v2.3-navigation-confidence
1. Select subjects by what this physical page meaningfully discusses, not by distinctive words alone. Keep primary and useful secondary subjects, including different subjects in separate works or notes. Use salience to reflect the depth and importance of treatment. A passing name, example or quotation is not automatically a subject: would a researcher seeking that subject find enough relevant discussion here? Do not imply a developed doctrine from a brief mention. There is no fixed subject count; sparse pages may have no concepts.
2. With Portuguese summaries, prefer concise, specific Portuguese subject labels understandable outside this page. Name the question or contextual concept, rather than copying an entire assertion or an unexplained lemma. Prefer the supported specific subject to a generic umbrella term. Retain original technical terminology when needed for precision, with verbatim expressions in evidence. A modern abstraction can faithfully name the page's meaning without occurring literally in its language. Apply grounding to semantic support, not lexical presence: explicit for directly expressed content, inferred for a defensible interpretive step. Do not project later doctrines, movements or complete theories onto the historical speaker.
3. A named person, institution, individual work, edition or chapter/volume designation alone is not a conceptual subject. Preserve useful persons, works and other named objects as appropriately typed entities; keep purely structural section information in its supplied metadata. A discussion of a work's authorship or authenticity may justify both a work entity and a concept for the problem discussed. Do not discard useful bibliography. Merge local concepts only when their contextual meanings are equivalent; related but distinct senses remain separate. Do not generate synonym lists, alternative translations or hypothetical search queries. Cross-page aliases and indexing expansion belong to a later stage.
4. For each stance edge, read the complete target proposition as what the source actor affirms, defends or refutes. Keep the opponent's thesis, the critic's reply and an allegation about the opponent separate. A rebuttal of a conclusion does not refute a premise the author concedes. Preserve a conditional objection as hypothetical, not an attested position of its named participant. If a target requires an implicit conclusion, qualify the inference or omit the edge; do not invent a doctrine. Bibliographic interprete or versio can indicate translation: use TRANSLATES to a WORK when supported, not INTERPRETS for exegesis.
5. In modes enabling relations, check that central identifiable participants and explicitly expressed debates survive as entities and coherent claims, rather than only inside concept meanings. Apply the existing identity, stance and work-boundary safeguards. Explain a specific unresolved voice instead of guessing. No minimum number of actors, claims, events or edges is required.
6. Parallel original/translation passages do not require duplicate evidence for every item. One informative, contiguous quotation may suffice. Add evidence when needed for identity, another argumentative step, a linguistic distinction or material disagreement. Never harmonize conflicting versions, silently repair OCR, stitch distant fragments or remove note markers from copied text. Do not shorten a quotation until its claim, condition or negation disappears merely to improve matching. Return only the existing extraction schema, without your internal subject-selection questions.
