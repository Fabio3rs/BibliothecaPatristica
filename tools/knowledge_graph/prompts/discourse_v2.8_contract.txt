DISCOURSE ATTRIBUTION
Before linking a claim, distinguish: discourse voice = who presents the statement; claim holder = who is represented as holding that exact position; grammatical agent = who performs the described action; quoted source = whose words/authority are invoked; target = what the stance concerns. Roles may differ. These are interpretation checks, not additional output fields.

Separate quoted/reported content from the narrator's description, interpretation or use of it. Attribute each proposition at its own level. A reporting verb can identify the holder of its embedded content, but does not make that person the holder of the entire reporting statement. A quoted authority does not inherit the surrounding commentator's conclusions. Preserve nested attribution and conditional/negated scope in proposition and mode when needed; do not flatten them into unconditional commitments.

For AFFIRMS/DEFENDS, require support that the actor endorses/argues for the exact target. For ATTRIBUTES_TO, require that the source assigns the exact claim to that holder. For REFUTES, identify the critic and the rejected proposition separately; the critic need not hold it. A mentioned participant or grammatical subject alone establishes none of these links.

If ownership or scope cannot be represented faithfully, retain the supported claim with its qualifications and evidence, omit only the misleading edge, and use AMBIGUOUS_ATTRIBUTION when ownership is unresolved. Keep supported relations and useful subjects; do not replace attribution errors with blanket omission.
