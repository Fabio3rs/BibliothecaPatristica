    XV. — OF THE SAME, FROM THE LETTER TO THOMAS HIS SYNCELLUS, IN
    WHICH HE SHOWS THAT IT IS THE SAME THING TO SPEAK OF THE UNION IN
    CHRIST AS FROM TWO NATURES AND FROM TWO HYPOSTASES.
  </bloco>
  <bloco tipo="nota_marginal" script="latino" bbox="40,585,80,600">
    513-8
  </bloco>
  <bloco tipo="texto_principal" script="latino" bbox="110,590,940,675">
    For those hypostases or natures, being in composition without diminu-
    tion, and not existing separately and in individual existence, make up one
    person of one Lord and Christ and Son, and one incarnate nature and hypo-
    stasis of the Word.
  </bloco>
  <bloco tipo="nota" script="latino" bbox="250,695,790,720">
    From the same letter after a citation¹ from Cyril.
  </bloco>
  <bloco tipo="texto_principal" script="latino" bbox="110,735,940,835">
    From what has been stated the doctor teaches that the peculiarity of the
    natural union is that the hypostases are in composition and are perfect
    without diminution, but refuse to continue an individual existence so as to
    be numbered as two, and to have its own person impressed upon each of
    them, which a conjunction in honour cannot possibly do.
  </bloco>
