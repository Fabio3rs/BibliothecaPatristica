    2nd book of those written during episcopacy, which was addressed
    To Conon the silentiary¹.
  </bloco>
  <bloco tipo="nota_marginal" script="latino" bbox="70,865,125,890">
    513-8.
  </bloco>
  <bloco tipo="texto_principal" script="latino" bbox="145,865,960,1215">
    The reason for which we are said to have become heirs of the curse and
    of condemnation and of death is not that the sin and condemnation and
    death passed to us, as if these fell to our nature by lot, for man's nature
    was from the beginning free from all these things, but that the method by
    which intercourse takes place derived its origin from sin, as I have said,
    a method which cut away the blessing of immortality, so that the race of
    men is preserved from dissolution by the procreation of children². We
    therefore were in consequence born mortal from a mortal father. These
    things are defined both by the holy John in the Commentary on the Epistle
    to the Romans³, and by the holy Cyril in the letter to Succensus⁴.
  </bloco>
