    The words that were written by the writer in the book, and these also
    about Job, of whom it is written that he also 'shall again rise with those
    whom the Lord raiseth'¹, we must suppose to have been composed with
    this meaning. In consequence of so great a trial he had in a sense gone
    down to Sheol and final destruction, so that even his name should thence-
    forth be extinguished, as he himself said when he was being tormented by
    the pains², and suddenly he arose as from the dead, and put off the
    unsightliness of the sores, and he was comely in body, as in the bloom of
    youth, and everything ended for him in a change to the best fortune. There-
    fore, lest any think that the prize of virtue ends with these things, and
    lest we who read imagine that we shall receive the rewards for the labours
