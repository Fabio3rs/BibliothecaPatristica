  </bloco>

  <bloco tipo="texto_principal" script="latino" bbox="120,550,930,810">
    But Isaiah² also the prophet, beholding beforehand with foreseeing eyes
    the holy order of the monks, who take upon themselves the voluntary
    distresses for the sake of the law of the Lord, said that their tribulation was
    offered to God in place of sacrifices and offerings, in that he wrote thus :
    « At that time there shall come up offerings to the Lord of Sabaoth from a
    people distressed and plucked »³. But the word ‘plucked’⁴ shows the
    abundance of the distresses; for, if one grasp the hair of a man’s head, and
    pluck this out by force, one causes him intolerable pain and injury. Where-
    fore also the great Job too, when he wished to show the abundance of his
    own pains, said, « Having grasped me by my locks he tore » (or « plucked »)
    « me »⁵. Accordingly therefore, since you have been marked for such a life,
    you ought to have souls ready and prepared for every trial that is thought
    to be hard.
  </bloco>
  <bloco tipo="aparato_critico" script="latino" bbox="120,830,930,900">

    published in Greek in S. V. N. C., IX, 729; but the words from ‘in that’ to the end of the citation
    following are there omitted, and the text is thereby rendered meaningless. — 3. Is., XVIII, 7. — 4. Read
    therefore τετλμμένου for τετμημένου in the Greek. — 5. Job, XVI, 13.
