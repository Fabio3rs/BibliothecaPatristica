    saying, « Keep thy foot when thou goest into a house of God, and draw nigh
    to hear ». For a man who is going along the road of religion must keep
    his own foot and be attentive lest he anywhere slip; and, when he is walking
    to the church as to a house of God, I mean the teaching of piety and the
    confession of the faith (for this is what we must suppose the church properly
    to be), draw nigh to hear, and not hold aloof, and apply and attach so to
    speak a ear that loves listening to the sound words of the teaching. For a
    mind that is so disposed God reckons in place of a sacrifice, and above every
    gift and fruit-offering of the foolish, who know not how to do good, nor yet
    will learn from those who know. Therefore he who said, « Keep thy foot
    when thou goest into a house of God, and draw nigh to hear », went on to
    say carefully, « Above a gift of the foolish is thy sacrifice, because they are
    not men who know how to do good »¹.
