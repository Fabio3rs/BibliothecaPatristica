    The introductory portion in A. giving information about Anba Pisentius'
    home, parentage, etc. contains some material not found elsewhere : especially
    we are told that he was for seven years in the monastery of Anba Maqâr
    south of mount Shama (= Jême, cf. fo. 102 b), W. E. Crum (in Z. d. Deutsch.
    Morg. Gesell. 1914, 176) suggests that Maqâr (مقار) is an error for بقام and thus
    the reference is to the Phoebammon monastery, and so in fo. 97 b. and in 106
    the place is named Psamer-Pshamer (بشمورة fo. 10 b). The greater part of
    his monastic life was spent in mount el-Asâs, i. e. Tsentî near the Dêr el-
    Bahri. « From the life of Andreas, we gather that the Gebel el Asâs em-
    braced the series of monasteries lying between Danfîḳ and Naḳâdah. Thus
    it would lie mainly, as the Synaxarium tells us, in the diocese of Keft. At
    the present day the name El Asâs seems however to designate generally
    the mountain between the Theban necropolis and the Tombs of the Kings
    and thus to have usurped that of « the Hill of Jême ». » (W. E. Crum in
    Winlock-Crum : Monastery of Epiphanius at Thebes, Part I, N. Y. 1926. 108).

  <bloco tipo="texto_principal" script="misto" bbox="80,405,920,550">
    Pisentius was born about A. D. 568 and was admitted a monk by Elias
    the head of the Phoebammon monastery at Jême, probably NE. of Medinet
    Habu. After dwelling some time in a « cave » or deserted tomb in the λαύρα
    of this monastery he moved to another which lay to the south of the monas-
    tery (cf. 103. a), and later again to a community on mount El-Asas (106. a).
    Elected bishop of Qift he was consecrated by the patriarch Damianus at
    Alexandria, probably in 598. He retired into the desert during the Persian
    invasion of 616-627.
  </bloco>

    Of the 56 « Wonders » in the A. text, 21 are peculiar to it (i. e. 2, 3, 18,
    19, 28, 29, 34, 36, 37, 40, 41, 42, 43, 45, 46, 47, 50, 51, 52, 55, 56). Of
    these, however, 2 may be only a variant of 33 which occurs also in B : pos-
    sibly 3 and 43 are variants of the same incident : 28 and 29 are closely
    related : 37, 51, and 52 may be variant accounts of 7 which occurs also in
    B. and S. : 42 and 47 seem to be portions of one narrative : whilst 31 (also
    in B. and S.) may be a variant of 39 (also in C.).
  </bloco>

    ted in A., only fo. 158.a-160.b. contain any passage of considerable length
    which is without parallel in A. The arrangement, however, is quite dif-
    ferent. Of the 56 wonders recounted in A. only 18 are given in C. but the
    repetitions and variants which serve to increase the A. text do not appear.
