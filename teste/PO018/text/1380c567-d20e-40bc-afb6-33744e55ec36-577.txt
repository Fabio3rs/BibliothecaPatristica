    were marking him the whole night. And, when he heard the sound of the
    bell, he stretched himself on his rug, and was rising with everyone else just as
    if he had been heavily sunk in his sleep. And, when the houseworkers had
    risen and recited the service¹, the same again went on till morning, and thus
    he would weep lying on his face, in order that he might not rise and show
    his labour to anyone, until his eyes became swollen and greatly inflamed.
    And in the morning, when the archimandrite² and the houseworkers also³
    saw his eyes, they say to him : « What has happened to your eyes, our
    father? ». But he on his side said to them : « The air (ἀήρ) of yesterday
    affected me on the road, and therefore my eyes swelled⁴ ». But those
    who had seen the manner in which he had been employed all the night
    continued saying « Leave the blessed man alone ». During the day he went
    out into the precinct of the monastery, and found a concealed spot, and
    there he bowed himself till the midday bell⁵, while they were seeking him
    all over the monastery; and then as they were going round they found him
    bowed down and weeping. They say to him : « Be so good⁶ as to go in, father;
    for the archimandrite is asking for you ». He came; and the archimandrite
