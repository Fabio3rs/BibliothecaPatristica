    on their side were alarmed and very greatly annoyed, saying, 'Man, who
    are you and what do you want? And why do you want to prove us here?
    And what do you want to learn from mime-actors (μίμος)?' And, because
    I recognised the trouble which their minds felt, as if they had already been
    laid bare, I gave them a great and terrible oath, 'No men shall hear from my
    mouth about you as long as you are in this city'. Then they were a little
    appeased, requiring at my hands that I should not be seen speaking
    with them by day or honour them or greet them, and should not be
    prevented from slapping (κοπτέσαι) them as mime-actors (μίμος) as they said.
    And I gave them this promise. Then that man begged that young woman,
