    their having taken possession of them and driven out our holy fathers who
    dwelt there, and killed many, and burnt the churches and the manshūbahs¹,

    monks were dispersed among the cities and villages and monasteries, in
    the various provinces of Egypt and the two Thebaids. Thus none was
    left in the cells of Wādī Ḥabīb save a few persons, who chose death, that
    they might redeem the life of their brothers by their own life, and so
    inherited eternal life by their endurance. And God protected them, so that
    none did them any further hurt or injury.
    At that time there was in the monastery of our father Macarius a priest,
    called James, who shed light around him by his conduct. When the de-
    vastation of the monasteries began, he quitted them and departed to a
    monastery in Upper Egypt, that he might serve God there, while awaiting a
    time when he might return to the holy mountain of Mīzān al-Kulūb, or Wādī
